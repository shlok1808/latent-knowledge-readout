## 4. Errors (reading says / paper says / evidence)

**E1. Fig 5, location of the strongest stripe (minor-moderate).**
- Reading says: "strongest stripe at the first key token ('Here')" (figures[Figure 5].numbers), "the largest is at the first token (a usual attention-sink position)" (text_matches_figure), and "the attention change is largest at the first token (an attention-sink position)" (pitfalls).
- Paper says (p13, Fig 5): the dashed correction line sits directly under the "Here" row, so the correction fires at the first generated token. The dark vertical stripe is on the second key token, "is".
- Evidence: I upscaled the p13 image and measured the fraction of dark-red pixels per key column. Panel (a): column "Here" = 8%, column "is" = 58%. Panel (b): "Here" = 5%, "is" = 38%. The paper's text (p13) only says "certain key tokens"; it never names the token.
- Fix: say "strongest stripe on the second generated token ('is'), the first token after the correction; other stripes at 'step', 'deriva[tion]' and a few later tokens". Drop "attention-sink position" (that is a guess, and the second token is not the usual sink). The remaining point stands: the correction fires on preamble tokens before any reasoning, and the two examples are hand-picked.

**E2. Scope of the "tuned on test" claim (minor).**
- Reading says (pitfalls[0]): "Hyperparameters for every method were swept on the evaluation benchmark itself; the reported Table 1 numbers equal each method's best configuration in Table 4."
- Paper says (p12, Table 4): the sweep is shown for Llama-3.1-8B on MATH-500 only. How the other 7 model-by-benchmark cells (Gemma, GSM8K, HumanEval, MBPP) were tuned is not reported.
- Fix: "On MATH-500 / Llama, the Table 1 numbers equal each method's best Table 4 configuration (MAGS 0.530, ITI 0.498, AS 0.506), which suggests test-set selection; tuning for the other cells is not reported." Also the paper never states that it selected on the test set; this is an inference from "best result per method in bold" (the reading's wording "(test-set selection)" is fine if marked as inference).

**E3. "Headline 10.8%" (minor).**
- Reading says: the claim "appears to be relative improvement; 0.530 vs 0.478 ... about 10.9%".
- Paper says (p2): "outperforms static steering baselines ... by up to 10.8%". The arithmetic 0.052/0.478 = 10.9% is correct, but that is gain over **unsteered**, not over static baselines. Over static baselines the gaps are much larger in the paper's own tables (Gemma HumanEval: MAGS 0.604 vs ITI 0.463, CD 0.433, i.e. +14 and +17 points), and those come from the baselines degrading.
- Fix: add "the 10.8% only fits the Llama MATH-500 gain over unsteered; against baselines the largest gaps are from baselines breaking (Gemma HumanEval)".

## 5. Omissions (paper overclaims the reading records but does not flag)

**O1. "Largest gains on MATH-500" is false for Gemma (key_results[4]).** Paper p7: "+5.2 ... Llama, +3.4 ... Gemma". But Gemma HumanEval is +4.4 (0.560 to 0.604), larger than Gemma MATH-500 +3.4. The paper's own text and tables disagree.

**O2. Table 4 "MAGS is insensitive" is overstated (Table 4 entry).**
- The gain appears only at alpha = 1.0 (0.530). Alpha 0.3/0.5/0.7 give 0.502/0.498/0.492, all inside the unsteered CI [0.428, 0.516]. The response is non-monotonic in alpha.
- The MAGS spread (0.492-0.530, 3.8 points) is about the size of the claimed gain (5.2 points), so "varies narrowly" does not hold relative to the effect.
- Top-1 and top-3 give identical accuracy at both shared alphas (0.492 at 0.7, 0.530 at 1.0). So the extra two heads add nothing, or never trigger, or it is a coincidence. This matters for us: it suggests the whole effect is one head, which makes the "which head" localisation both more interesting and more fragile.
- The reading says text_matches_figure "yes for ranges". Suggest adding these three points.

**O3. "Highly separable" (contribution 1, p2) vs Fig 3.** Best single head AUROC about 0.85; dotted heads sit at about 0.65-0.75; most heads are near 0.5. No multi-head classifier and no simple baseline detector (trace length, mean token confidence) is given. The reading's pitfalls note "no correction for 128 heads" and aggregation inconsistency, but not that "highly separable" is not what Fig 3 shows. One extra confound the reading does not mention (my observation, not in the paper): max-aggregation of per-step scores over variable-length traces is biased by trace length, and wrong traces are often longer. Worth a length-matched check if we reuse the AUROC map.

**O4. Remark 1 is asserted, not tested.** The paper has no pooled-PCA vs difference-PCA ablation. The reading's bears_on_group_angle says per-item differencing "is essential" and controls_worth_copying lists it with no caveat. Fix: "paper asserts, does not test; we should run the pooled baseline ourselves". Also, Eq. 4-5 is a compact SVD of the stacked difference vectors and the paper never mentions centring, though it says the subspace captures "greatest variance". As written, the top direction follows the mean flip-minus-resist shift, not the variance across items. The reading calls this "PCA"; hedge it ("PCA/SVD, centring not stated").

**O5. Fig 4 cannot show "drift into the error subspace".** It plots only the KDE of correct-output activations, with mean trajectories averaged over all problems (right and wrong together). No error-trace density is shown. So the reading's Fig 2 remark "real data in Fig. 4 is far less cleanly separated" is a loose comparison; Fig 4 has no error cloud to separate. Suggest rewording to "Fig 4 never shows the error class".

**O6. Perplexity claims (p8).** The paper says ITI and AS "generally raise perplexity". Its own tables: Llama MBPP ITI 2.250, AS 2.214, CD 2.291 are all far below unsteered 2.978, while MAGS 2.970 is about unchanged. Gemma GSM8K: every steering method is below unsteered 1.710 (ITI 1.293, AS 1.416, CD 1.107, MAGS 1.210). Gemma MBPP ITI 5.053 < unsteered 5.169. On Gemma HumanEval MAGS (1.281) is worse than ITI (1.267) and CD (1.272). The reading catches the GSM8K Gemma MAGS point but not that the claim about baselines is contradicted. Also this means the unsteered perplexity itself looks unreliable and the scorer is unspecified (the reading notes that last part).

**O7. Table 3 (off-focus, minor).** MAGS^u is indistinguishable from MAGS (validity identical 54.8; binding -7.56 vs -7.54), so the table does not support the compositional (MAGS^u) claim. 500 molecules, no CIs. The reading's methods list does not discuss MAGS^u.

**O8. Label wording.** bears_on_fates: override is rated "moderate". The paper contains no override evidence; "moderate" is about method fit as a template. Clarify "moderate (as a template), no evidence from the paper".

## 6. Ideas worth keeping (to make sure we get the best from this paper)
Already in the reading and sound: per-item contrastive differencing at head outputs (flipped minus resisted on the same fact); threshold calibrated on resisted runs; conditional projection removal as a fate-4 Stage B baseline next to DPO; forced-decoding attention-shift map as a fate-3 (not routed) routing check; per-head AUROC map for flip vs resist; use both AUROC tails and a signed score for fate 5.
Two small additions worth recording:
1. MAGS^u idea: fit one subspace per pressure type (pushback vs tempting wrong option) with disjoint head sets, then compare principal angles and test whether the union fixes both. This ties directly to H1 and H2, even though the paper's own evidence for it is weak (O7).
2. Add the controls the paper lacks, as part of the template: random-subspace control, always-on vs conditional (same alpha), paired test on the same items, and a length-matched AUROC (O3).
