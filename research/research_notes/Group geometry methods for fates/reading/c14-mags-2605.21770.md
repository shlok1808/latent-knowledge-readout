# Manifold-Guided Attention Steering (MAGS)

**arXiv 2605.21770v1, 20 May 2026.** Li, Satish, Guruprasad, Sengupta, D'Antoni, Yu (UC San Diego). The paper links no code.

## In one line
For each attention head, the method learns the few directions in which failed reasoning runs differ from successful runs of the same problem. During generation it removes that component, but only when the component is large. The reported gains are small.

## Relevance to our project (honest)
Less relevant than the title suggests. The "manifold" is a linear PCA subspace with no curvature, no tracking across layers and no group structure. The useful parts are engineering pieces for Stage A/B: per-item differencing at head outputs, conditional projection, and an attention-shift check under forced decoding. The evidence is weak (hyperparameters tuned on the test set, overlapping CIs, figures that only partly support the text). Use it as a method template, not as a result.

## Setup
- **Models:** Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Gemma-4-E4b-it for math and code. GPT-OSS-20B for SMILES only.
- **Benchmarks:** MATH-500 (500), GSM8K (1319), HumanEval (164), MBPP (427), and SMILES (500 molecules, scored for validity and docking).
- **Fitting data:** sampled traces from training sets (Math-Instruct, GSM8K train, APPS), up to 8 samples per problem. Problems without both a correct and a wrong trace are dropped.
- **Site:** per-head attention output (the input to W_O) at every generated token. The diagnostic map covers Llama layers 8, 16, 24 and 31.
- **Method:** subtract each problem's mean correct-trace activation from its mean wrong-trace activation, then run PCA over problems to get the subspace B. The score is ||B(a_t − mu_c)||². When the score passes a threshold, subtract alpha·BᵀB(a_t − mu_c).
- **Not reported:** the rank k, the percentile q, the number of training problems, the decoding temperature, and which heads were steered.
- **Baselines:** ITI, Angular Steering, Contrastive Decoding, unsteered.

## Key results (with quotes)
- Claim: heads "diverge from a low-dimensional correctness manifold at the point of error, and this deviation compounds" (p1).
- "by up to 10.8%" (p2). This looks like a relative gain: 0.530 vs 0.478 on Llama MATH-500.
- "The signal is sparse and concentrated in specific heads (marked dots for AUROC > 0.65)" (p5).
- "+5.2 points over the unsteered Llama baseline and +3.4 points over the unsteered Gemma baseline" on MATH-500 (p7). HumanEval rises "by approximately 4 points" (p7).
- "MAGS accuracy varies narrowly (0.492–0.530)", while "ITI spans a much wider range (0.214–0.498)" (p8).
- After a correction, "certain key tokens receive consistently higher attention across all subsequent query steps" (p13).

## Methods we could use
1. **Per-item contrastive subspace per head.** For each fact, take the flipped-run mean minus the resisted-run mean (or post-pushback minus pre-pushback), then PCA. Compare the resulting subspaces across pressure types with principal angles (H1). If they align with a "user-claim" direction, that points to override. Cheap; it needs facts with both outcomes under sampling.
2. **Per-head AUROC map** for flip vs resist, used to find heads that read the user's claim. Use both tails and a signed score.
3. **Conditional projection removal** as a Stage B fix for override, compared with plain DPO (H2). Add a random-subspace control and an always-on version.
4. **Per-item token trajectories** in subspace PCs, plotted over a density of resisted runs, to find fork points.
5. **Attention-shift map under forced decoding.** After restoring the correct answer, check whether later heads actually read it (the *not routed* check).

## Controls worth copying
- A problem-level train/test split (p5).
- Per-item differencing before PCA (Remark 1). Otherwise the top PCs are just item content.
- Thresholds calibrated only on good (resisted) runs.
- Steered and unsteered runs on the same forced token sequence, "so the two caches diverge solely through the corrections" (p13).
- Baselines re-fit on the same contrast sets, with a side-effect metric next to accuracy.

## Pitfalls
- **Tuned on test.** Table 4 sweeps every method on MATH-500, and Table 1 reports each method's best configuration (p12).
- **Weak statistics.** The CIs are unpaired and overlap almost everywhere (p14). There is no paired test.
- The labels are trace-level (correct final answer, p3) and the means are over all tokens. So the subspace does not locate a "point of error".
- Aggregation is inconsistent: max for the diagnostic and mean for head selection (p5). The threshold rule also differs (balanced accuracy vs q-th percentile).
- k, q, the size of the training set, the temperature and the chosen heads are never given.
- Selection bias: only problems with both outcomes within 8 samples are kept (p7).
- The score is an unsigned norm around one global mean. It cannot see sign flips, so it is no use for *transformed*, and the inverted (AUROC < 0.5) heads are ignored.
- From the figures (approx), corrections seem to fire at the first generated tokens, and the biggest attention change is at the first token (an attention sink).
- "Three model families" is a stretch: reasoning and code use two models, and GPT-OSS appears only in SMILES.

## Limitations and future work (authors)
- "MAGS requires a set of contrastive-output traces for manifold fitting, which may be difficult to curate in low-resource domains." (p9)
- "the degree to which the learned manifold transfers across tasks within a domain ... remains an open question" (p9). They also list dynamic head selection and modelling heads jointly.

## Group / subspace angle
- **Helps:** per-item differencing before comparing point clouds; head-level subspaces instead of the residual stream; cheap pictures of trajectories and fork points; a cheap routing check.
- **Warns:** "manifold" here is just a linear subspace. Mean trajectories hide per-item forks, the unsigned norm throws away direction, and 128-head scans need multiple-comparison care.

## Where in the architecture
Attention-head outputs (before W_O). In Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, signal appears at layers 8, 16, 24 and 31, densest in the last layer (~15 of 32 heads) with the strongest single head in layer 8 (approx, from Fig 3). That is one model and one dataset. The steered heads for Gemma and GPT-OSS are not reported, and there is nothing on seeds or checkpoints.

## Actionable fix
A head-level conditional projection learned from per-item flipped-vs-resisted pairs and triggered by a threshold set on resisted runs. It is a cheap fate-4 baseline to set beside DPO, plus a per-head monitor. Treat it as a baseline to beat.

## Verdict
**Adapt.** Copy the per-item differencing, the head-level subspaces, the conditional projection and the forced-decoding attention check. Don't copy their evaluation practice, and don't cite the paper as evidence that correctness lives on a manifold.
