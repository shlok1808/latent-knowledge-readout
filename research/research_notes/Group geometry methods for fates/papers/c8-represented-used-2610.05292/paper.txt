What happens to information a pretrained
model already encodes when post-training no
longer rewards using it? The common language
of representation compression conflates three
fates: information may be erased, rerouted
away from the decision while still represented,
or rescaled to occupy less variance while still
represented and used. We make these fates
identifiable in models whose pretraining recovers Bayesian belief states. A reward that
reads only a coarse function of the hidden state
defines an exact reward-null kernel. The kernel lets us separately measure whether the information remains recoverable, whether decisions causally depend on it, and how much
activation variance it occupies. Theory says
what is protected: KL-anchored reinforcement
learning preserves the reference policy’s logodds among equally rewarded outputs, supervised and unanchored objectives carry no
such constraint, and spectral compression implies neither erasure nor loss of use. In controlled worlds, post-training mostly reroutes or
rescales reward-null information and leaves it
decodable. Without an anchor decisions can
stop using it although the representation survives, and with one they keep using it. Erasure
appears only under prolonged weight decay,
for distinctions that neither reward nor nexttoken prediction can see. Open language models show the same dissociation: in-context belief geometry stays decodable under late-layer
spectral compression, and within-class behavior depends on the anchor. Post-training thus
selects a causal quotient of the pretrained belief
state: the reward defines decision-equivalence,
the anchor and the state update protect part
of what it ignores, and optimization decides
whether the rest is erased, rerouted, or rescaled.

a task variable with a nuisance variable, the belief state is known exactly, and a reward that reads
only the task variable defines in closed form the
reward-null kernel: distinctions that pretraining
has reason to encode and reward has no reason to

protected before any network is trained. The optimum of KL-regularized reinforcement learning
is πβ∗ ∝ πref eq/β (Korbak et al., 2022; Rafailov

stay sensitive to it. Supervised and unanchored
objectives carry no such constraint, and spectral
statistics are invariants of neither information nor
its use. We therefore ask three separate questions of
each reward-null distinction: is it still represented,
does the policy still use it, and how much variance
is allocated to it?
The resulting picture is sharper than a generic
account of post-training as compression. Rewardnull information is usually retained after its role in

the policy changes: what next-token prediction can
see stays decodable under all practical objectives
while its variance contracts, and the anchor decides
whether the decision keeps reading it. Genuine erasure appears only under persistent pressure such as
prolonged weight decay, for distinctions protected
by neither reward nor prediction. A recurrent filter can keep encoding a distinction it has stopped
using. What the reward leaves open, optimization
dynamics resolve: without an anchor, policies settle on within-class behavior far from the reference
model’s, all of it equally rewarded.
The same dissociation appears in open language
models. Post-training leaves the in-context belief
geometry, including components invisible to the
next token, decodable while late-layer spectra contract, and under coarse-class GRPO within-class
preferences leave the base policy without the anchor and return to it with one. Post-training thus
selects a causal quotient of the belief state, the
distinctions its decisions still act on, and erased,
rerouted, and rescaled are distinct, empirically identifiable fates of reward-null information.

Proposition 2 (Anchor identity). For every β > 0:
(i) if two actions have equal expected reward at
h, their log-odds under πβ∗ equal their log-odds
under πref ; (ii) if b(h) ∼R b(h′ ), the difference

Rafailov et al., 2023; GX-Chen et al., 2026). What
we use is its consequence for the belief state. Many
outputs of a language model are equally correct,
the reward cannot separate them, and the reference
is the model’s own next-token distribution. Our
main design reproduces this situation: every token is an action, the reward reads only the token’s
class, and the reference prefers among the tied tokens of a class according to the nuisance belief.
The optimal policy then depends on the belief only
through (bκ , b⊤ E). Its causal quotient merges exactly the beliefs that differ along Ninv , at every β,
and Nvis must stay represented and used at the decision. In centered log-probabilities the optimum is
C log πref +Cq/β, so the variation within a fiber is
untouched while the variation between fibers grows
as 1/β. The kernel keeps its log-odds and loses
its share of the variance: at the output, anchored
reinforcement learning is rescaling by construction.

The filter protects what future decisions need.
The second constraint comes from how the state is
updated. When the observations entangle the two

decision reads a state updated one token at a time,
must then carry the reward-null distinctions that
future reward-relevant beliefs depend on, whereas
a transformer can recompute the belief from the
context at the decision.

A Recurrent State Spreads Freedom and
Protection

A GRU decides from the state it carries along the
sequence, so the decision and every other position
share one representation. What the objective frees
is then lost everywhere, and what it protects is kept
everywhere. Under objectives that free the kernel
the GRU retains less of the next-token-invisible
component than the transformer and loses it at ordinary positions too. Under an anchor in the weightdecay stress test it keeps the component whole
(0.99 to 1.02, against 0.64 in the transformer), because the anchored log-odds at the query need the
full nuisance belief to arrive through the recurrent
state.
Exchanging the kernel component of the state
between twins at an ordinary position, and reading
the next-token distributions that follow, measures

what the model’s own filter uses, relative to the
exact Bayes filter under the same exchange. The
base GRU uses the kernel exactly as Bayes does.
After SFT its filter all but stops using the invisible
component (0.01 of the base) although half of it is
still decodable from the same state: rerouting, now
inside the computation (Figure 3). An anchor keeps
part of that use, and in the entangled world, where
the kernel feeds future reward-relevant beliefs, the
filter keeps using it under every objective. A recurrent filter keeps what a future reward-relevant
belief needs and drops what nothing needs.

Optimization Settles What the Reward
Leaves Open

Without the anchor the objective is silent about tied
outputs, and two modes of the policy gradient compete. A history-wise mode amplifies the preference
that each history already has within a class, as a
policy with separate parameters for every history
would. A collective mode shifts the within-class
preference of all histories in the same direction, because shared features couple their gradients. At the
reference nothing drives this shift, but the reference
is an unstable equilibrium for it: any asymmetry
of the network grows, and it grows only while the
reward is still being learned. Which mode leads
when the reward saturates depends on how the representation at the query divides its second moment
(Appendix A.4).
In the separable worlds the collective mode wins.
The slope γ first rises above 1, as the history-wise
mode predicts, and the shift overtakes before the
reward saturates (Figure 5, II). Two controls identify the mechanism: training only the head removes
the collapse, so the shift is written into the shared
representation, and the exact expected gradient collapses as well, so sampling noise does not cause
it. The outcome is the same across pretraining and
post-training seeds (Appendix D.1). Where the two
modes grow at comparable rates at the base model,

Related Work

Belief states in sequence models. Our object
comes from computational mechanics (Crutchfield
and Young, 1989; Shalizi and Crutchfield, 2001)
and from the finding that transformers represent it
linearly (Shai et al., 2024; Piotrowski et al., 2025),
in context in open language models (Balcells et al.,
2026). We ask what a second objective, which
needs only a quotient of that state, does to what
next-token prediction installed.

7

Conclusion

A reward that ignores part of a belief state does
not decide what happens to that part. The reward
defines which distinctions are decision-equivalent.
The anchor and the state update determine which
reward-null distinctions remain protected: the anchor keeps what the reference policy distinguishes,
and the filter keeps what future reward-relevant beliefs need. Optimization decides the fate of the
rest. It usually rescales it, reroutes the decision
around it when nothing anchors the decision, and
erases it only under sustained pressure and only
where no term of the objective needs it. Read
through the three coordinates, a compressed spectrum stops being evidence of loss, and the question
of whether post-training compresses a representation gives way to the question of which causal
quotient of the belief state it implements.

Post-training and representation change. Geometric and spectral studies compare checkpoints
before and after post-training (Wang et al., 2026;
Li et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2026), and mechanistic
studies find fine-tuning to wrap or enhance existing
circuits (Jain et al., 2024; Prakash et al., 2024; Lee
et al., 2024). In all of these the distinctions the
objective renders irrelevant are unknown, so a measured change cannot be assigned to loss, rerouting
or rescaling. The exact quotient supplies that assignment, and Proposition 1 explains why spectral
statistics alone could not.
KL-regularized reinforcement learning and tied
outputs. That the KL-regularized optimum preserves the reference’s proportions among equally
rewarded outputs is known (Korbak et al., 2022;

The exact ground truth that makes the three fates

The language-model experiments cover three pairs

one controlled objective on in-context synthetic

the anchor carry over to pretrained language models, and the size of either effect in post-training on
natural tasks is unmeasured. Represented information is measured with linear probes, so a distinction
that survives only in a nonlinear code is counted as

for the difference. What makes amplification the
cheaper direction in a pretrained language model

Worlds, Models and Training

Worlds. Transitions stay in the current state with
probability 1−x and move uniformly otherwise. In
the separable family the task chain has x = 0.1 and
emits its own symbol with probability 0.85. The
nuisance chain has three states and two symbols:
two states emit their own symbol with probability
αF and the third emits either with probability one
half, so the emission has rank two and one direction
of the nuisance belief is invisible to the next token.
The main world uses xF = 0.15 and αF = 0.95,
a second point xF = 0.05 and αF = 0.99, and
the slow world xF = 0.02 and αF = 0.85. The
control replaces the nuisance emission by an invertible one, which makes the whole kernel visible.
The entangled world emits a pair: the sum of the
two variables modulo three with fidelity 0.85, and
a copy of the task variable with fidelity 0.75, which
is needed to make the task variable identifiable.
The Mess3 world uses the parameters of Shai et al.
(2024) with the classes {A} and {B, C}, which
leaves a one-dimensional kernel. A query token is
inserted after an observation with probability 0.05
from the eighth observation on, and the next observation follows it, so the hidden state does not
depend on the query or on the action.

The direction without the anchor

The collapse does not depend on sampling noise,
on the tying of the head, or on the optimizer family.
The exact expected gradient collapses more completely than GRPO (intercept 6.1 nats). An untied
head with a trainable trunk collapses like the tied
head, with the same slopes within seed noise, and
the collapsed log-odds are written in the residual
stream. Training only the head does not collapse
within 5,000 steps, and its trajectory is predicted
without free parameters by the fixed-feature model
of Appendix A.4. SGD collapses more slowly than
AdamW. A next-token loss does not prevent the
collapse in transformers. The setting matters in
practice because current recipes for reinforcement
learning with verifiable rewards mostly remove the
KL term (Yu et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025; Hu et al.,
2025), after earlier ones used coefficients of 0.04
and 0.001 (Shao et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025),
which is the range in which the policy-level threshold of Section 4.2 lies.
Parameterization and budget do not change the
direction (Figure 14). Low-rank adapters of rank
4 and 16 collapse in nine of nine runs and remove
