# 2402.14811: Fine-Tuning Enhances Existing Mechanisms (Prakash, Rott Shaham, Haklay, Belinkov, Bau; ICLR 2024)

Read: full paper.txt (main text + appendices A-J), figure pages p08 (Fig 3) and p09 (Fig 4). No web.

## SIMPLE
The task is entity tracking. Example: "The apple is in Box F, ... Box F contains the" -> "apple". There are 7 boxes, and chance is 0.14. They study Llama-7B and three fine-tunes of it: Vicuna-7B (chat), Goat-7B (arithmetic, LoRA) and FLoat-7B (arithmetic, full fine-tune). Accuracy is 0.66 / 0.67 / 0.82 / 0.82. With path patching they find a 72-head circuit in Llama-7B, in four groups. D (Structure Reader) and C (Position Detector) read the query box. B (Position Transmitter) carries the *position* of the right object to the last token. A (Value Fetcher, about L15-L24) uses that position to fetch the object. So the model tracks a pointer (position), not the object's content.

They take the same Llama circuit unchanged and test it in the fine-tunes. It still recovers 0.97 / 0.89 / 0.88 of full-model accuracy, while random circuits score about 0. Circuits found directly in Goat and FLoat are larger (175 heads). They are roughly a superset of the Llama circuit, and every group has the same job (DCM desiderata). Cross-model activation patching (CMAP) means copying one group's head outputs from Goat or FLoat into Llama on the same input. Copying the Value Fetcher outputs alone lifts Llama from 0.66 to the fine-tuned level (about 0.82). Copying the Position Transmitter outputs gives a big boost too (about 0.78 Goat, 0.74 FLoat). Conclusion: fine-tuning makes the existing route stronger and adds helper heads. It does not build a new algorithm.

## HELPS US
- **Stage B, removed vs hidden (H2):** do CMAP between base and fix checkpoints (DPO vs fate-matched) on the same pushback input. Patch group by group and see which sub-step the fix changed. If copying only the "correct-route" heads from the fixed model into the base model fixes the base, the fix strengthened that route. Then test whether the competitor heads still behave the same in the fixed model, for example by reusing the base model's competitor heads, frozen, and checking whether ablating or activating them still moves behavior. If they do, the fix hid the problem.
- **H3, frozen vs refit:** the paper's main move is to find the circuit in the base model and test it unchanged in the tuned model. That is the circuit version of our frozen vs refit probe check. Their activations moved across models without problems (a 7B fine-tune with the same base). This supports frozen probes as a first pass across checkpoints. Still log refit vs frozen, because DPO might shift subspaces more than arithmetic SFT does.
- **Transformed / bypass fates (H3):** here the key variable is a *position pointer*, not the answer's content. A content probe ("is Canberra represented?") would miss a route that carries "where the answer was in context". Add a position-style desideratum to Stage A. Swap where the original answer sits and check whether the flip follows the position. That test tells "transformed" apart from "not routed".
- **Override fate, competitor ablation:** in the appendix, keeping only the Goat circuit and mean-ablating all other heads *raises* Llama to 0.77, above its full 0.66 (Table A5). They guess negative heads cause this. Use it as a cheap override test: under pushback, mean-ablate everything outside the correct-answer circuit. If accuracy recovers, a competing signal exists outside the circuit. This shows causal control only, not endogenous mediation.
- **WHERE to look:** attention heads at specific token positions in mid layers (about L9-L24 of 32 at 7B). The last-token "fetcher" heads read from the answer token's position. For pushback, compare last-token heads that read from the model's first answer position with heads that read from the user's "I'm sure it's Sydney" span.
- **User's takeaway: partly holds.** Correct: fine-tuning made the existing route stronger and added helper heads, so "DPO may strengthen the right route" is a fair prior. Not shown: anything about a *competing* route staying. This task has no conflict and no pressure. The fine-tunes were arithmetic SFT and chat SFT, not DPO or preference training. The negative-head point is a speculation in the appendix. "Competing route stays, so check for hidden" is our hypothesis to test. The direct evidence for masking is 2604.19117 (ablation effect grows after the RLHF refresh), not this paper.

## CAVEATS
- One synthetic task (box tracking, single-token objects). Only Llama-7B fine-tunes. No conflict, pushback, or factual recall. No 9B-27B models. Training type is SFT/LoRA, not DPO or RL.
- Their "same circuit" rests on faithfulness (0.88-0.97 for the arithmetic tunes). The Llama circuit is also not complete (Table A1), and backup heads may hide parts of it. They never check whether any head *lost* its function or was suppressed. That is exactly the removed-vs-hidden question, and they don't answer it.
- Every result is causal patching (what heads *can* do when patched in). Nothing shows endogenous mediation on natural inputs. CMAP success also depends on the two models sharing a base, so it may not hold after heavy RL.
